Before a tenant moves in

If you lean towards renting, these are the Queensland basics. NGU’s property management team works through them with you.

  • Smoke alarmsInterconnected photoelectric alarms are required in every rented home in Queensland.
  • Minimum housing standardsApply to every tenancy since 1 September 2024.
  • Rent reviewsOnce every 12 months, and the limit stays with the property, not the tenant.
  • Landlord insuranceIn place before the lease starts.
  • Entry condition reportThe new kitchen, carpet and shutters photographed and on record from day one.
  • The six-year ruleWorth your accountant’s view before you move out.

Questions you might have

Why a range rather than one number?

A desktop appraisal is built from records, not from walking through the home. Seeing the finish, the light and the outlook in person usually narrows it.

What could push the sale price up?

Presentation, photography that sells the outlook, and timing a launch so the home is not one of several similar ones at once. Most of the four-bedroom homes asking around $1 million this week sit on blocks over 1,000 m², so a renovated home with a view would stand apart.

What could hold it back?

A softer market, or several renovated homes launching in the same weeks. And buyers who want everything brand new will price in the bathrooms; buyers who like the retro tile won’t.

Can we rent it now and sell later?

Yes. A lease in place stays with the property if it sells, and inspections are arranged around the tenant. If a sale within a year is likely, a shorter lease keeps your options open.

Does this commit us to anything?

No. It is an appraisal, not an agreement. When you have decided which way you are leaning, we talk through that direction properly.
